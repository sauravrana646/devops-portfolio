Admission controllers become theater when the only people who understand them are the two who wrote the first ClusterPolicy. Durable policy is readable, tested, and fixable in a PR.

## Prefer intent over cleverness

Name policies after the risk they reduce. “require-signed-images” beats “policy-17b”. Put examples of failing and passing manifests in the same repo as the policy.

## Fail closed on the few things that matter

If everything is denied, teams invent bypasses. Start with unsigned artifacts, privileged pods in prod, and wildcards that mint cloud admin. Expand only when the first gates are boring.

## Close the loop in CI

Surface the same checks before merge. Catching policy at deploy time teaches people to hate security. Catching it in the PR teaches people to keep it.

For supply-chain work, that often means the same checks in CI that policy will enforce later—so unsigned or unsafe images never reach a cluster through a side door. See [Secure CI/CD is a path, not a scanner](/blog/promotion-gates-signed-releases/).
